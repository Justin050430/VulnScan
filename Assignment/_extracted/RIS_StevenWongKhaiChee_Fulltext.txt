Abstract
This project aims to develop a secure file encryption tool using the AES256 encryption standard
to prevent unauthorized access to sensitive data. The tool is designed with a user-friendly
interface and incorporates the zero-knowledge principle, ensuring that sensitive information, such
as user passkeys, is never stored. A web-based environment is employed to facilitate testing,
initially focused on TARUMT university students, with a goal of evaluating the system’s
effectiveness and usability.
The project follows the Agile development framework, which allows for flexibility and iterative
improvements throughout the planning, analysis, design, implementation, and testing phases. The
system design includes advanced cryptographic techniques, robust key management, and a
client-side encryption model that guarantees data privacy. The implementation involves the use of
the AES256 algorithm, paired with a secure hashing technique for key generation, all executed
within the user’s browser to maintain adherence to the zero-knowledge principle.
In addition to the technical development, the project is validated through comprehensive testing,
including unit, integration, and user acceptance tests, ensuring functionality, security, and user
experience across different devices and browsers. The absence of server-side data handling
enhances privacy and security, making the tool a significant contribution to cybersecurity. Future
research opportunities lie in expanding the tool's functionalities, such as supporting larger files
and conducting more extensive vulnerability assessments.
This project contributes to the field of encryption technology by providing a practical, scalable,
and secure platform for file encryption, offering both theoretical insights and practical solutions
for secure data handling in the digital age.

Introduction
1.1 Background
Encryption has become a standard practice in today's digital world to keep various aspects of
computer systems secure. Almost every technology we use involves data encryption, especially
when transferring data, managing network traffic, and protecting information. So how does
encryption work? Encryption works by converting data from a readable format into an unreadable
encoded format (Kaspersky, 2020). When data is in this unreadable format, no one can understand
its content, keeping it protected and hidden. The encrypted data can only be read or processed
after it's been decrypted, usually using a personal passkey known only to the user who initiated
the encryption process.
Encryption is essential in today's digital landscape for safeguarding sensitive information and
ensuring secure communication channels. After all, encryption is a foundational element of
cybersecurity (Poller, 2023). Encryption prevents unauthorized access and protects data privacy,
including personal, financial, and proprietary information, thus preserving data confidentiality. It
serves as a critical defense against cyber threats, reducing the risk of data breaches and identity
theft. Moreover, encryption facilitates compliance with regulations and industry standards
governing data protection, fostering trust among customers and stakeholders.
This project will be focusing on symmetric encryption as it is widely used in various aspects of
our modern world to secure sensitive information and communications. For instance, in online
banking and e-commerce transactions, symmetric encryption ensures that financial data, such as
credit card numbers and personal details, is protected from unauthorized access during
transmission over the internet. Similarly, in messaging applications and email services, symmetric
encryption is employed to safeguard the privacy of communications between users, ensuring that
messages remain confidential and cannot be intercepted by malicious actors.
Moreover, symmetric encryption plays a crucial role in securing data stored on electronic devices,
such as computers, smartphones, and portable drives. By encrypting files and folders with
symmetric encryption algorithms, individuals and organizations can prevent unauthorized access
to their data in case their devices are lost, stolen, or compromised.

1.1.1 Symmetric Encryption
Symmetric encryption is a type of encryption key management solution where only one key (a
secret key) is used to both encrypt and decrypt electronic data. ((Smirnoff & Turner, 2020). In this
method, the sender and receiver share a single secret key, which is kept confidential between
them. When the sender wants to encrypt a message or data, they use this secret key to transform
the plaintext into ciphertext, which appears as gibberish to anyone who intercepts it without the
key. Upon receiving the encrypted data, the receiver uses the same secret key to decrypt it back
into its original plaintext form.
One of the main advantages of symmetric encryption is its efficiency and speed, making it
suitable for encrypting large amounts of data. Additionally, because only one key is required for
both encryption and decryption, symmetric encryption is relatively straightforward to implement
and computationally efficient. Symmetric encryption provides a powerful and efficient means of
securing data communication and storage, particularly when combined with secure key
management practices. It is widely used in various applications, including secure communication
over networks, data storage encryption, and protecting sensitive information in databases.
1.1.2 AES Encryption Standard
The AES Encryption algorithm (also known as the Rijndael algorithm) is a symmetric block
cipher algorithm with a block/chunk size of 128 bits and It utilizes different key lengths (Jena,
2023), which can be either 128 bits, 192 bits, or 256 bits, depending on the particular variant
chosen. The algorithm works by dividing the data into blocks and applying a series of
substitution, permutation, and mixing operations to each block. These operations are performed in
multiple rounds, with the number of rounds depending on the key size used (128-bit, 192-bit, or
256-bit). AES is known for its robustness, efficiency, and speed, making it suitable for a wide
range of applications, including securing data in transit over networks, encrypting files on storage
devices, and protecting sensitive information in databases. AES has become the de facto standard
for encryption and is widely adopted by organizations and industries worldwide due to its strong
security features and compatibility across different platforms and systems.

1.1.3 Zero-Knowledge Encryption
Zero-knowledge encryption has been around for a while, but its use has increased in the last few
years, this is particularly true for consumer data storage services (Ware, 2022).
Zero-knowledge encryption keeps your data safe and private when using services like storing files
online or managing passwords. Your data is scrambled using keys made from your main
password and secret keys stored on your device. The app never keeps your data in plain, readable
form, and the service's servers never get it that way either. This means only you can unscramble
your data. Even if there's a security problem with the service, your data stays safe because no one
else,
not
even the service provider, knows your password. This is why it's called
zero-knowledge—only you know the secret (Ware, 2022). One of the way of how it is being
applied on Cloud service is like when a user create a password on Cloud service, the
password will be generated a hash value from their device to the Cloud Service to be
saved, means that the Cloud service provider only knows the hash value of the password
but not the original password written by the user, now only the user know the password.

1.2.2 Insecure Encryption Protocol
Existing encryption tools or services widely use symmetric standards for encryption, which, while
effective, may not provide complete security. In many cases, service providers store users'
credentials, such as personal passkeys, which poses a significant risk of compromise. For
instance, if a service provider's database is breached, attackers could potentially gain access to
users' passkeys, undermining the security of encrypted data. Potential vulnerability in the current
era is that with the attack on data breach is still ongoing, the data breach could expose millions of
users' passkeys, leading to unauthorized access to encrypted files and compromising users'
sensitive information. One of the great ways is to implement zero-knowledge encryption so no
passkey will be stored on the server's side. However, many popular cloud storage services, such
as Google Drive and Dropbox, don’t use zero-knowledge encryption and retain access to your
files. (Wolford, 2023). Some companies don't apply the zero-knowledge principle because they
want to make managing files easier and avoid wasting data space if clients forget their credentials
for encrypted data, resulting in a less secure encryption protocol to protect the user's passkey.
1.2.3 Incompatibility of Encryption tools
As more people rely on electronic devices and transition to a paperless lifestyle, the need to
secure sensitive information stored on phones or computers becomes increasingly critical.
Individuals often save confidential data, such as banking passwords or health records, within their
device's operating system, posing a significant risk if the device is compromised. Despite the
availability of numerous encryption tools on the market, many users encounter challenges due to
incompatibility of device’s setting in running encryption service, discouraging them from
prioritizing encryption for security. For example, a file that's been encrypted from a smartphone is
sometimes not supported and can’t be decrypted on a software application on a computer, and the
other way around. A large portion of executives don't want their data encrypted because they
view encryption as being too complicated to use.
(Rebecca , 2020). For instance, popular
encryption tools like VeraCrypt offer robust encryption services but are hindered by complex
settings and overwhelming sub-functions, leading to usability issues and a reluctance to adopt
encryption practices.

1.3 Objective
1.3.1 To develop an encryption platform for encryption & decryption of files
using AES256 standard
The primary objective of this project is to design and implement an encryption platform capable
of securely encrypting and decrypting files using the AES256 standard. This platform will utilize
advanced cryptographic techniques to ensure the confidentiality and integrity of sensitive data.
Specific features of the platform will include user-friendly interfaces for file selection, encryption,
and decryption processes, as well as robust key management mechanisms to safeguard encryption
keys. Additionally, the platform will support various file formats and sizes, enabling users to
encrypt a wide range of file types securely..
1.3.2 To employ zero-knowledge principle in encryption and decryption
management.
In addition to implementing AES256 encryption, this project aims to incorporate the
zero-knowledge principle into the encryption and decryption processes. In the context of
encryption, this principle will enhance security by ensuring that the encryption platform does not
have access to users' plaintext data or encryption keys. Instead, encryption and decryption
operations will be performed locally on the user's device, with the platform facilitating the
exchange of encrypted data without compromising confidentiality. By leveraging zero-knowledge
techniques, the encryption platform will provide an additional layer of protection against
unauthorized access and data breaches, reinforcing the overall security posture of the system.
1.3.3 To deploy the developed encryption platform in the web-based system to
assess its effectiveness in safeguarding sensitive data.
Once the encryption platform is developed, the next objective is to integrate it into a web-based
system and evaluate its effectiveness in safeguarding sensitive data in real-world scenarios. The
deployment phase will involve implementing the encryption platform within a secure web
application environment, allowing users to upload, encrypt, and securely store files online.
Through extensive testing and user feedback, the performance, usability, and security features of
the encryption platform will be assessed to ensure its effectiveness in protecting sensitive data
against various threats, including unauthorized access and data breaches. Additionally, measures
will be taken to address any potential vulnerabilities or usability issues identified during the
deployment phase, with the ultimate goal of delivering a reliable and user-friendly encryption
solution for web-based applications.

2.1.1 Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) algorithm in IoT security system.
Based on the literature review of the journal article titled “IoT security system with modified
Zero-Knowledge Proof algorithm for
authentication”, the modified Zero Knowledge Proof
algorithm contributes to authentication in data transmission in IoT security systems by improving
the performance and security of the authentication process. The algorithm enhances the speed of
the authentication process, with the proposed method showing better performance compared to
previous methods. It reduces the authentication process to approximately 5 milliseconds from
both the client side and server side, which is a significant improvement over the previous method
that took between 1-15 seconds for authentication.
Additionally, the modified Zero Knowledge Proof algorithm increases the range of values and
calculations, with the values being much larger calculations so breaking into the security will be
much more difficult(Soewito & Marcellinus, 2020). This is crucial for IoT security systems,
especially for continuous data transmission, as it provides a higher level of security. The
algorithm also introduces a one-time token mechanism, where the token data transmission
changes with each transmission, further enhancing security.
In summary, the modified Zero Knowledge Proof algorithm significantly improves the speed,
security, and range of values for authentication in data transmission in IoT security systems,
making it a valuable contribution to enhancing the overall security of IoT networks.

14

method employs a block cipher system and can perform symmetric key encoding operations with

In the context of image file security, the AES method is applied to secure image files from

and security of the encryption process.
During the decryption process, the encrypted image file is decrypted using the AES method with
the corresponding key and operating mode. The decrypted image is then displayed, and the
original image file is reconstructed from the encrypted data.
Overall, the AES method is utilized to ensure the confidentiality and integrity of image files by

4.2.1 Understanding Client-Side Encryption
Client-side encryption means that all encryption and decryption processes are carried out on the
user's device, specifically within their browser. This approach ensures that sensitive data, such as
files and passwords, do not leave the user’s device in an unencrypted state.
Example of Client-Side Encryption Web Application flow:
1.
User Uploads a Raw File: The user selects a file and uploads it. Despite saying "upload,"
in client-side encryption, this file does not actually go to a server. Instead, it is read
directly from the user's file system by the browser.
2.
User Enters a Pass Key: The user types in a pass key. This key is used locally within the
browser and is not sent to any server.
3.
Generation of Hash Value: The web app generates a hash of the pass key (still within the
user's browser). This hash value is used as the encryption key for the AES256 algorithm
but does not leave the user's device or browser.
4.
Encryption Process: The web app encrypts the file using AES256 with the hash value as
the key. All of this processing happens in the user’s browser.
5.
User Downloads the Encrypted File: The now encrypted file is available for the user to
download directly to their device. No encrypted file is sent to or stored on the server.
6.
Decryption Process: To decrypt, the user must provide the original pass key again locally,
which the app hashes to recreate the encryption key. The decryption process also occurs
entirely in the browser.

5.3.1 Encryption Process Implementation
The encryption process involves converting the original file data into an encrypted format that is
unreadable without the correct decryption key. Here's a step-by-step breakdown of how the
encryption process is implemented:
70

Figure 5.4: Code snippet for encryption process.
STEP 1: Key Derivation using PBKDF2
The first step in the encryption process is to derive a strong cryptographic key from the
user-provided passkey. This is achieved using the Password-Based Key Derivation Function 2
(PBKDF2). PBKDF2 takes the user's passkey and a randomly generated salt and produces a
32-byte (256-bit) key suitable for AES-256 encryption. The salt is used to ensure that even if two
users choose the same password, their derived keys will be different. PBKDF2 is generally more
preferable than SHA-256 for key derivation because PBKDF2 is specifically designed to be
computationally intensive and slow, using multiple iterations (e.g., 1,000,000) and a salt to
generate a cryptographic key. This makes it highly resistant to brute-force and dictionary attacks.
In contrast, SHA-256 is a fast cryptographic hash function, which makes it vulnerable to rapid
brute-force attacks if used directly for password hashing or key derivation.
Figure 5.5: Code snippets for key derivation using PBKDF2
●
Salt Generation:
A salt is a random 16-byte value generated for each encryption operation. This random
value is combined with the user's password to make the derived key unique.
●
PBKDF2 Iterations:
The count parameter is set to 1,000,000, which means the key derivation function runs for
1,000,000 iterations. This significantly increases the computational effort required for
brute-force attacks, enhancing the security of the derived key.
STEP 2: AES Encryption Setup
After deriving the key, the application initializes an AES cipher in CBC (Cipher Block Chaining)
mode. CBC mode requires an Initialization Vector (IV), which is a random value that ensures that
the same plaintext will encrypt to different ciphertexts every time, provided the IV is different.
The iv is a 16-byte random value generated by the cipher object. It is critical for ensuring the
uniqueness of ciphertexts and is stored alongside the encrypted data.

STEP 3: File Data Preparation and Padding
To ensure the security and integrity of the encrypted file, the original file extension is prepended

Summary
This project aimed to develop a secure web-based encryption tool named StevHash that utilizes

privacy and security. The project was implemented using Python with Flask for the backend and

iterative development and refinement, ensuring flexibility and responsiveness to emerging
requirements and challenges. Overall, the project successfully delivered a robust and user-friendly

​7.2
Achievements
The project achieved its primary objectives by developing a functional web application that
provides secure file encryption and decryption using AES-256. It effectively applied the
zero-knowledge principle by ensuring user passwords are never stored on the server, only the
generated hash values are temporarily used. The tool was tested rigorously through unit,
integration, system, and user acceptance testing to validate its performance, security, and
usability. Strengths of the system include its strong encryption method, ease of use, and secure
handling of user data. A noted weakness is the lack of support for large files beyond a certain

Contributions
StevHash brings innovation by combining strong AES-256 encryption with zero-knowledge
protocols in a web-based environment, offering a user-friendly interface that simplifies secure file
handling for users with varying technical skills. This system is necessary as it provides a secure
way to encrypt and decrypt files without exposing user data to potential breaches, making it
highly relevant in the age of increasing cyber threats. Its marketability lies in its applicability for
individuals and organizations looking for a secure, easy-to-use encryption tool that does not
compromise user privacy.

​7.4
Limitations and Future Improvements
While StevHash effectively addresses the need for secure file encryption, there are several
limitations that need to be addressed in future developments:
1.
Insufficient Testing:
Although the web application has undergone basic functional testing, it lacks a
comprehensive Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing (VAPT) assessment.
Conducting VAPT is crucial to identifying potential security weaknesses and ensuring the
safe operation of the application in various threat environments. Future improvements
should include a thorough VAPT to strengthen the security posture of StevHash.
2.
User Interface Limitations
Currently, the user interface (UI) of the StevHash web application is optimized only for
desktop and laptop devices. It does not support smartphone interfaces effectively, leading
to usability challenges on smaller screens. Future development should focus on making
the UI responsive and adaptive to different screen sizes and devices, enhancing
accessibility and user experience across all platforms.
3.
Limited File Size Support
The current implementation of StevHash supports only small to medium-sized files,
approximately up to 2GB, which restricts its use for encrypting large datasets. To
overcome this limitation, future improvements could focus on optimizing the encryption
process to handle larger files efficiently without compromising performance or security.
4.
Exploring Advanced Encryption Algorithms
Encryption technologies continue to evolve, and future iterations of StevHash could
benefit from integrating more advanced encryption algorithms such as XChaCha20.
XChaCha20 offers several advantages over AES-256, including better performance on
some platforms, resistance to timing attacks, and more robust handling of nonce misuse.
87

Implementing XChaCha20 could enhance both the security and performance of the
application.
5.
Enhanced Key Management
While the current version of StevHash employs a basic key management mechanism
using PBKDF2 for key derivation, there is a need for more secure and scalable key
management solutions. Future improvements could involve implementing hardware
security modules (HSMs) or integrating with external key management services (KMS)
to provide enhanced security, better key rotation policies, and improved overall key
management.
These future improvements would not only enhance the functionality and security of StevHash
but also expand its applicability to a broader range of use cases, ensuring it remains a robust and
reliable encryption tool for modern data security challenges.

Issues and Solutions

implementing secure key management and ensuring the zero-knowledge principle was
maintained. These were addressed by employing well-documented cryptographic libraries and
following best practices in secure coding. The user interface required refinement to ensure
accessibility and usability across different devices, which was solved through iterative testing and
user feedback. Lessons learned include the importance of agile practices for accommodating
evolving requirements and the need for continuous security assessment to address potential
vulnerabilities.
